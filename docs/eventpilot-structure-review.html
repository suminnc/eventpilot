EventPilot Agent Structure — Review

Proposed: 1 Exec owner · Logistics lead + one sub-agent per category · 1 Finance gate · autonomous, cross-checking.

Verdict — keep the skeleton, fix three things first. A single accountable Exec with a Logistics hub and an independent Finance gate is the right topology, and the “lead scopes → fans out per category” pattern is exactly what a workflow DAG is good at. But gifts is orphaned, “lowest cost” has no revenue lever and attendee experience has no executor, and “checking each other” is cosmetic until decision rights and a veto are defined — otherwise the agents will just agree with each other.

What already works

One throat to choke

The playbook’s #1 failure mode is an unowned thread. A single Exec that owns “did it get done” fixes that.

Logistics as the hub

Matches the ops playbook — Logistics is the team that turns every other commitment into a physical thing on event day.

Finance as an independent gate

Logistics wants quality, Finance wants cost. That tension is your “balance quality and cost” mechanism, and it’s cheap to run.

Lead scopes, then fans out

Per-category sub-agents keep parallelism bounded and let a small event spawn fewer agents (no merch agent if there’s no merch).

Gaps & concrete fixes

GapFix
blocker Gifts is orphaned. You listed gifts in Logistics’ goal but only four sub-agents (food, schedule, venue, merch). Either fold gifts into merch (rename it Merch & Gifts) or add a 5th sub-agent. Prizes and speaker gifts have different lead times than swag and often need sponsor coordination + tax forms → route those to Finance.
blocker No revenue owner. “Keep cost as low as possible” gives Finance exactly one lever: cut. The experience pays for it. Add a Sponsorship agent (revenue + deliverables) whenever sponsors exist; otherwise Exec owns it. Without a revenue line, the budget is a hard ceiling, not a constraint you can trade against.
blocker Attendee experience has no executor. Exec owns the priority but nobody produces the attendee guide or the run-of-show narrative. Make the “Schedule” agent the Program & Run-of-show owner (agenda + attendee-facing schedule), and give Exec — or a thin Comms agent — the attendee guide, reminders and on-site comms.
naming “Schedule” is ambiguous. It collides with the T-minus planning timeline. Rename to Program & Run-of-show. Keep venue owning the floorplan; the two reconcile at each walkthrough.
control No decision rights, so “checking each other” is decorative. Write the RACI: Exec = scope, priority, over-budget approval; Logistics lead = what’s needed and vendor choice; Finance = affordability veto; sub-agents = options and trade-offs. Any disagreement surviving 2 rounds escalates to an Exec decision memo.
control “Unless absolutely necessary” is undefined. Make it a number: over-budget or >5% variance needs Exec sign-off, a 10–15% contingency line, deposits capped at 50%, and no spend without a signed quote or contract.
control Vendor scam risk has no owner. Logistics lead runs the vetting checklist (new vendor / international / deposit > $500) with Finance as second sign-off: verify identity, card/escrow never a wire to a changed account, call back on a known number, proof before final payment.
state Two teams, two ledgers → drift. One source of truth per artifact: Projects Tracker (Logistics), Budget Ledger (Finance), Deal Sheet (Sponsorship). One owner per row; Finance reconciles ledger vs committed spend every cycle and flags the difference.
loop Unbounded negotiation. Cap vendor negotiation at 2 rounds, then the lead recommends and Exec decides. Cap fan-out to applicable categories only.
verify Nothing independently checks the output. Add a verification pass before Exec approves: every row has owner / date / vendor, dates checked against the T-minus gates, and no unsigned deal counted as spend or revenue.

The autonomous control loop

  1. Exec sets scope. Must-haves vs nice-to-haves for the attendee experience, plus a budget envelope.
  2. Logistics lead scopes the event. Produces the required-category list and the tracker skeleton — only categories that apply.
  3. Sub-agents run in parallel. Each returns options, 3 quotes on identical spec, a quality-vs-cost trade-off and a recommendation.
  4. Finance gates. Budget-line check, deposit cap, payment method, over-budget flags — approve or send back with a reason.
  5. Lead reconciles everything into one plan; unresolved quality-vs-cost goes to Exec as a decision memo.
  6. Verification pass. Independent check of ownership, dates against gates, and unsigned spend.
  7. Exec approves and publishes the this-week action list.

How this becomes a workflow agent

exec_scope → logistics_plan (lead scopes + assigns) → 4 parallel category tasks (food, venue, program, merch_gifts) → finance_gate → reconcile → verify → exec_approve. Sponsorship and Comms sit beside Logistics as their own branches when they apply. This is a bounded DAG — no human input needed mid-run, and the Finance gate plus the verification pass are what actually make the agents check and balance each other.
